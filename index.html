Concept Unlearning via Cross-Attention Activation Subspaces in Diffusion Models

A closed-form edit built from layer-specific cross-attention activations rather than a shared text-space basis. CASE suppresses target concepts while preserving neighboring concepts, with no fine-tuning or inference-time overhead.

01

Overview

Existing closed-form concept-unlearning methods usually derive editing directions from fixed text embeddings, which do not capture how a concept changes across latent states, timesteps, and layers. CASE (Cross-Attention Subspace Erasure) constructs layer-specific forget and retain subspaces from cross-attention activations collected during denoising, then folds a retain-constrained linear operator directly into the cross-attention K/V weights. Across 10 concepts in Style, IP, Celebrity, and NSFW, CASE achieves the highest H-Mean among the evaluated baselines in every category. It also remains effective under recovery attacks, jointly unlearns up to 100 artistic styles, and extends to SDXL and FLUX.1-dev.

02

Why activation bases?

We build a basis from a small set of short anchor prompts, either from text-encoder embeddings or from cross-attention activations, then train a binary classifier in that basis. We measure recall on a held-out set of natural prompts that describe the same concept in longer, more varied form than the anchors.

A text-space basis catches only a small fraction. A cross-attention activation basis achieves approximately five times higher average recall across Style, IP, and Celebrity.

This is the core motivation for CASE: build the editing basis from how the conditioning signal is expressed inside the denoising computation.

03

Method

Cross-attention in the diffusion U-Net. At every cross-attention layer \(\ell\), image features form queries \(Q^\ell\), and the text embedding \(e\) is projected into keys and values by learned weights \(W_K^\ell\) and \(W_V^\ell\). The post-attention activation at one query position is

$$h^\ell = \mathrm{softmax}\!\left(\frac{Q^\ell {K^\ell}^{\!\top}}{\sqrt{d^\ell}}\right) V^\ell.$$

\(W_K^\ell\) and \(W_V^\ell\) determine how text content flows into the image. CASE edits exactly these matrices given a small forget anchor set \(\mathcal{A}_F\) and a retain anchor set \(\mathcal{A}_R\). The procedure has three steps, requires no gradient-based fine-tuning, and adds no computation at inference time.

STEP
01
ACTIVATION COLLECTION

Run a short denoising trajectory

For each anchor prompt, run a short denoising trajectory with a few random latents. At every cross-attention layer \(\ell\) and every step, read the post-attention activation and mean-pool over the spatial axis.

Stack the rows into per-layer activation matrices \(H_F^\ell\) (from forget anchors) and \(H_R^\ell\) (from retain anchors).

STEP
02
SUBSPACE ESTIMATION

SVD on activation matrices

Take the SVD of \(H_F^\ell\) and \(H_R^\ell\), keep the top right-singular vectors up to a cumulative-variance threshold, and form orthonormal bases \(V_F^\ell, V_R^\ell\) and projectors

$$P_F^\ell = V_F^\ell {V_F^\ell}^{\!\top}, \qquad P_R^\ell = V_R^\ell {V_R^\ell}^{\!\top}.$$
STEP
03
CLOSED-FORM EDIT

Left-multiply the K/V weights

Compose the edit operator and apply it once to each layer's cross-attention key and value matrices:

$$E^\ell = I - P_F^\ell\,(I - P_R^\ell)$$
$$W_K^\ell \leftarrow E^\ell W_K^\ell, \qquad W_V^\ell \leftarrow E^\ell W_V^\ell.$$

The operator leaves vectors in the estimated retain subspace unchanged while removing forget-subspace components outside it. Because its bases are collected across latent states, timesteps, and layers, the edit better covers unseen formulations of the target concept.

Relationship to CURE. CASE uses an analogous forget-and-retain spectral construction, but estimates a separate subspace for each layer from cross-attention activations rather than building one shared basis from text-encoder embeddings.

Ablation: anchor set size

How sensitive is the method to how many anchor prompts we collect? We sweep the size of the forget anchor set \(|\mathcal{A}_F|\) and the retain anchor set \(|\mathcal{A}_R|\) independently and measure target detection on held-out prompts (lower is better) and retention of related concepts (higher is better).

Natural forget prompts improve suppression for both representations, but the text-basis edit loses retention as the forget set grows. With more retain anchors, the target rapidly reappears under the text basis. The activation basis keeps stronger suppression while retention improves and then plateaus near the default retain-set size.

06

Scalability, robustness, and generalization

The expanded evaluation tests whether the activation-space edit holds beyond the ten single-concept HUB settings. CASE maintains a favorable suppression-retention trade-off when jointly erasing up to 100 artistic styles, resists recovery attempts across prompt, embedding, and sampling attacks, and extends beyond Stable Diffusion v1.5.

100styles
Multi-concept scalability CASE jointly erases \(N \in \{1, 10, 50, 100\}\) artistic styles without retain anchors. It achieves the highest \(\mathrm{CLIP}_R - \mathrm{CLIP}_E\) at every evaluated concept count, indicating the strongest suppression-retention trade-off among CASE, CURE, MACE, UCE, and AdaVD.
4attacks
Robustness to recovery attacks On NSFW recovery, CASE records 34.7% under UDA and 4.0% under MMA, the lowest attack success rates in both settings. It reaches 23.2% under CCE, second only to SAFREE, and ties MACE for the best NBP result at 3.3%.
2larger models
Generalization across architectures On the IP category, text-derived edits leave target generation close to the original model across much of the threshold range. Activation-derived edits produce substantially lower target proportions on FLUX.1-dev and increasingly strong suppression on SDXL as \(\tau_F\) rises, while retaining comparatively high performance on neighboring concepts.
